# Build a Betterplan Playbook

A **Playbook** is a reusable Story Map for a repeatable process, authored once
and then instantiated as a new Endeavor. This skill produces the **Playbook
Definition**: the authored source — reference material plus one YAML Story Map —
that `betterplan-compile-playbook` compiles into the Template Betterplan loads.

## Who this is for

A **Playbook author**: a domain expert encoding a process they already know, who
arrives with material. Not an end customer looking for a template to use.

**Material is a precondition, not a nice-to-have.** If the folder is empty or
holds only a sentence of intent, stop and say so: a Playbook built from an
interview alone encodes one person's recollection, and the research step later
has nothing to check it against. Ask them to come back with the regulations,
standards, process docs or notes that actually govern the process. Do not
substitute a few orienting questions for source material.

## Input and output

**Input** — a folder the user names, holding whatever exists: PDFs, standards,
regulatory texts, process docs, notes, recordings.

**Output** — a Playbook Definition folder:

```
<playbook-slug>/
├── playbook.yml        # title, version, language, icon, description
├── storymap.yml        # the whole Story Map, one file
├── references/         # transformed source material
└── output/             # generated by the compiler; never hand-edited
```

The format is specified in
`betterplan-compile-playbook/references/playbook-format.md`. Read it before
writing either YAML file.

Publishing is **out of scope**. Write the folder where the user asks; committing
it, reviewing it, and deploying it are theirs. Do not run git.

## The process

### Step 1 — Gather, and settle the language

Read everything in the folder. Establish: the stated goal, what material exists,
what is missing.

Then settle one thing before anything else, because it shapes every title you
will write: **what language is the Playbook content in?** It follows the source
material and the eventual users, not the language of this conversation. Record it
in `playbook.yml` as `language:` and hold to it. This skill's own prose is
English; the Playbook's content need not be.

### Step 2 — Transform materials

Convert every reference document into something an agent can work with:

- PDFs → Markdown, preserving structure, tables and headings
- Images and diagrams → described in text
- Links → cached content or summaries
- Raw notes → structured notes

Write the results to `references/` as `NN-topic.md`. Keep the originals where
they are.

### Step 3 — Analyse

From the transformed material, extract:

- actors, steps, constraints, decision points, milestones
- the **backbone**: the chronological or logical sequence of steps
- where the process **branches**: variants, options, jurisdiction-specific paths
- gaps and contradictions

Map what you found onto the building blocks — Initiatives (goals or phases),
Epics (process steps), Stories (options and tools), Workitems (details). Keep
this provisional; Step 4 will move things.

### Step 4 — Interview the author

Interview them relentlessly until you reach shared understanding. Map the process
as a **design tree**: every decision branches into the decisions hanging off it.

Work in **rounds**. The **frontier** is every decision whose prerequisites are
already settled — the questions answerable *now* without guessing at answers you
have not heard. Ask the whole frontier in one round, numbered, each with your
recommended answer. Then wait.

```
❓ **Q1** - **<title>**: <question, with options where they exist>

➡️ <your recommended answer>

---

❓ **Q2** - **<title>**: …
```

Each round of answers reshapes the tree: settled decisions push the frontier
outward. Recompute it and ask the next round. A question whose answer depends on
another still-open question belongs to a **later** round.

Finding **facts** is your job, never theirs — if a question needs something from
the material or the filesystem, go find it. The **decisions** are theirs: put
each one to them and wait.

What the interview is for: pressure-testing the goal and scope, confirming the
sequence, surfacing exceptions and variants, naming every stakeholder and what
they want, resolving the contradictions from Step 3, and finding the real-world
constraints that shape the process.

The interview is done when the frontier is empty — every branch visited, nothing
silently assumed. Do not start writing `storymap.yml` until they confirm.

### Step 5 — Check against the field

Research the known best practices, standards and reference processes for the
domain. Compare what you have against what the field recommends, and surface
every deviation: a Playbook that quietly departs from the standard its users
expect is worse than one that departs loudly. The author decides what to adopt.

### Step 6 — Write the Story Map

Write `playbook.yml` and `storymap.yml` per the format reference. Then apply the
patterns below — they come from real Betterplan Playbooks and keep the result
recognisable.

**Initiatives: phases or goals?** Ask which fits.

- **Phases** — numbered chronological stages ("1. Vorbereitung & Entscheidung", "2. Notar & Gründung"). Good for linear processes with a clear start and end.
- **Goals** — outcomes that span the lifecycle ("Know what the regulation requires", "Ongoing obligations are met"). Good when streams run in parallel or repeat.

Record the choice as `initiativePattern:` so a later revision stays consistent.
Every Initiative gets a 1–2 sentence purpose statement.

**Epics: one step, one focus.** Each names a single coherent step, and its
description says what happens and why it matters in 1–3 sentences.

**Options become sibling Stories.** When a step has several valid ways to reach
the same outcome, each is its own Story under that Epic: Rechtsform wählen →
[Einzelunternehmen, GbR, UG, GmbH]; Buchhaltung → [lexoffice, sevDesk, DATEV].
The Epic groups them, the Stories distinguish them, and whoever instantiates the
Playbook picks one.

**Story titles: pick one style and hold it.** Record it as `titleStyle:`.

- **Outcome-oriented** (result): "Rechtsform gewählt und begründet", "Firmenname bei IHK geprüft". Answers *when am I done?*
- **Activity-oriented** (action): "Anforderungen festhalten", "Risiko bewerten". Answers *what do I do?*

Default to outcome-oriented — a Playbook's reader is usually trying to find out
whether a step is finished.

**Description depth by level:**

| Level | What to write | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Initiative | Purpose — what outcome does this phase or goal achieve? | 1–2 sentences |
| Epic | What happens here, why it matters, key constraints | 1–3 sentences |
| Story | User-story line, Context with rationale and references, acceptance criteria | Short to medium |
| Workitem | A checklist line. No description. | One line |

Regulatory and standards-heavy domains need rich Context sections citing the
legal basis; practical how-to domains stay short. Let the domain decide.

**Tags** are metadata only — never repeat their content in the description. They
must be namespaced `project:<value>` with exactly one colon, and every value
needs a color in `playbook.yml`'s `tags:`. Do **not** write `progress:*` tags:
the server assigns the starting phase per type, and a hard-coded `progress:idea`
puts every Workitem in the wrong column. See
`betterplan-workflow/references/data-model.md`.

### Step 7 — Iterate

Compile (`betterplan-compile-playbook`) and walk the result through with the
author: confirm the backbone order, add missing steps, adjust priority
top-to-bottom, merge or split Stories, review tags. Each round edits the YAML and
recompiles. Repeat until they confirm it is complete.

Edit `storymap.yml`, never the generated JSON — the next compile overwrites it.

### Step 8 — Version it

Set `version:` in `playbook.yml` and bump it on every substantive revision.

Be explicit with the author about what versioning does **not** buy them:
instantiating a Template copies its Story Map and regenerates every id, so **a
Playbook revision can never reach projects already created from the previous
one.** The version records which revision a project came from. There is no
migration path, and none is planned.
